# Phase 1.5 — Target-Disease Literature Validation: EYE FAMILY

Gate: >=3 independent papers/sources per target confirming a real target-disease link.
All 3 targets below PASS.

## 1. Carbonic Anhydrase II - Glaucoma
- Mechanistically, CA-II inhibition suppresses aqueous humor production by the ciliary processes, directly reducing intraocular pressure (IOP). (PMC13317502)
- Established treatments already exist: topical and oral carbonic anhydrase inhibitors (acetazolamide, methazolamide) are standard glaucoma therapy. (AJO 1989 RCT; PMC11795524)
- Glaucoma is the second leading cause of blindness worldwide and IOP is the only modifiable risk factor - direct clinical relevance confirmed across multiple independent studies. (Int J Peptide Res Ther, 2020)
- Status: PASS

## 2. VEGFR-2 (Vascular endothelial growth factor receptor 2) - AMD / Diabetic Retinopathy
- Anti-VEGF therapy (ranibizumab, bevacizumab, aflibercept, faricimab - all signaling through VEGFR-2/VEGFR-3) is the gold-standard, first-line treatment for wet AMD and diabetic macular edema. (PMC9409486, PMC12154301)
- AMD and diabetic retinopathy are major causes of vision loss in aging populations (AMD affects ~50M people worldwide); strong mechanistic and genetic support for VEGF/VEGFR-2 pathway centrality. (PMC12154301)
- Multiple independent trials (YOSEMITE/RHINE, TENAYA/LUCERNE, Protocol W) confirm clinical efficacy of blocking this pathway. (PMC11584429)
- Status: PASS

## 3. Aldose Reductase (AKR1B1) - Diabetic Retinopathy / Cataract
- Mechanistically the rate-limiting enzyme in the polyol pathway; hyperactivity drives sorbitol accumulation causing osmotic stress in the lens (cataract) and retina (retinopathy). (ACS Omega; PMC11047946)
- Gene-knockout studies (ALR2 deletion) protect against diabetes-induced cataract, retinal pericyte loss, and blood-retinal barrier breakdown in animal models. (PMC3317655)
- Caveat: despite decades of study, aldose reductase inhibitors have NOT yet demonstrated clinical benefit for diabetic retinopathy in humans, likely due to non-selective inhibition given AR's dual protective/pathogenic roles - a translational caution, not a target-validity failure. (Diabetology 2026, doi:10.3390/diabetology7030042)
- Status: PASS (mechanistic + genetic + animal-model validation; human clinical translation still elusive)
